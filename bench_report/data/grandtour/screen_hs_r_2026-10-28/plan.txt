Eighteenth HS screen, step 1: how far the registration moves a scan away from the IMU prediction (2026-10-28,
written before these runs).

Why: submission 5 (HS-G15g) scored 0.0426 m on the test, with arc-2 at 0.102. HS-G15g's own arc-2 output (no reference
read) jumps up 0.63 m in 0.6 s at t = 143 s (vertical speed 1.86 m/s, impossible for the walking robot), where the
Livox submissions stay level: the registration slipped in height in the ARCHE building. No development mission shows
such a jump with HS-G15g (the largest |vz| on the dev ARC missions is 0.98 m/s for a 0.23 m step on arc-3's stairs).
The idea is a GT-free guard that bounds how far the registration may move a scan from its IMU prediction (in height
first) and keeps such scans out of the map. Its threshold must come from development data only, with margin.
rko_lio 3403690 logs each scan's predicted (initial-guess) position with its registered one (output_filter_log 'U'
events; no output changes).
Runs: HS-G15g + output_filter_log (runs/g15glog.yaml, ws/rko_g33, offline_node md5 9dbf743c...) on the ARCHE
development missions arc-3 (stairs), arc-1, arc-4, arc-5, arc-6. Each must give HS-G15g's trajectory (hsq_g15g_c30 or
the G15g replays). Reported: per mission the distribution of the per-scan vertical and horizontal innovation
(registered - predicted): 99 %, 99.9 %, max, with where the largest occur. No test mission is run.

Interim (2026-10-28, arc-3 and arc-1 analysed): the per-scan innovation (registered - predicted) is large in normal
operation on these stairs: |dz| 99 % 40-50 mm, max 104 (arc-3) and 129 mm (arc-1); |dxy| max 49 and 85 mm. The
prediction starts from the registration's own velocity, so a slip would hardly show in it. The output filter's
innovation (IMU-driven prediction) is of the same size (|dz| max 82-122 mm). arc-3 rises 0.44 m and falls again at
78-80 s (outside the reference): integrating the IMU alone from 77 s gives the same rise (-2.60 -> -2.16 m by 78.5 s)
and fall, so it is real motion on the stairs, not a slip.
Amendment (before any further run): to know whether submission 5's arc-2 jump (+0.63 m in 0.6 s at 143 s) is a slip
at all, the test mission arc-2 is run once more with the same file plus output_filter_log (runs_test/g15glog.yaml,
ws/rko_g33): only its sensor streams are read, as in every test run, and only our own log is analysed (the IMU's
vertical motion against the registered one around 143 s), as with the frame tilt of the moving starts. Its trajectory
must equal submission 5's arc-2 byte for byte. Nothing from it is submitted; whatever guard follows is set on
development data.

Step 2 (fixed before any of its runs): G15gi = HS-G15g + registration_innovation_limit_xy 0.25 m and _z 0.25 m (rko_lio
82da285, ws/rko_g34; guard/g15gi.yaml): about twice the largest innovation of the five ARCHE development missions
(129 mm vertical, 85 mm horizontal). The value was set knowing the arc-2 slip (510 mm); any limit from 0.13 to 0.5 m
would catch it, and 0.25 m is the round value at twice the development maximum, not fitted to it.
Runs: G15gi on the 15 HS development missions and snow-1; each run reports how many scans the limits bounded.
Adopt G15gi as the GrandTour HS configuration in place of HS-G15g if all hold:

What it can and cannot show: on development data the guard can only be shown harmless (it was not needed there);
that it helps rests on the arc-2 diagnostic (guard_test/: the same file plus output_filter_log on the test's arc-2,
GT-free: is the slip bounded, and does the height then follow the IMU), not on any score. A COMFORT submission with
it would be a change made after seeing the test result of submission 5; whether to make one is the maintainer's
decision, stated as such.
