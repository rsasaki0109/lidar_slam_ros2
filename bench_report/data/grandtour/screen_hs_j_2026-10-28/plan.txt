Tenth screen on the HS rig: the start hold without the per-interval bias update (2026-10-28, written before any of
these runs; the ninth screen's last runs are still going).

Why: the eighth screen's ss (start-standstill hold) takes SNOW-3 0.0268 -> 0.0170 m and helps the static starts, but
loses ICE-1 (+3.5 mm) and ARC-6 (+2.7 mm). The ninth screen's levelling does not repair them (lvh ice-1 0.0187, arc-6
0.0124) and loses the others (lvh snow-3 0.0250, arc-5 0.0217): the tilt is not the cause (ARC-6 is tilted by only
0.2 deg). What ss also does is replace the IMU biases with each 0.1-s standing interval's means: the robot sways while
it stands (gyro 2-5 mrad/s on SNOW-3's stand), so the gyro bias becomes the last interval's sway, far above the
STIM320's; a moving start had left the biases at zero, which for this IMU is closer to the truth. rko_lio d42056f adds
start_standstill_bias: "interval" (as before), "average" (mean over all standing intervals so far), "none".
Variants (ws/rko_g23; HS-G15c + start_standstill_radius 1.0 + start_standstill_hold true; not tuned):
  h0 = start_standstill_bias none (hold and zero velocity only);
  ha = start_standstill_bias average.
Missions: snow-3, ice-1, arc-6, arc-5, arc-4 (moving starts), heap-1, pil-2, arc-1, arc-3 (static). Compared with
HS-G15c and the eighth screen's ss. A screen: a candidate it picks is judged on all 15 HS missions under a rule fixed
before those runs.

Not using the per-interval bias removes most of ICE-1's loss. But the hold took the IMU-predicted pose for a held
scan: with the biases kept and the frame still tilted by the moving start (ARC-5: 16 deg), that prediction carries
2.7 m/s^2 of misplaced gravity, and with the velocity reset each interval the position walked off by about 1.4 cm a
scan; the per-interval bias had hidden this by absorbing the tilt. rko_lio 5e99906 makes the hold keep the
position (the attitude follows the gyro); the screen was stopped and goes on as the eleventh with that build.
