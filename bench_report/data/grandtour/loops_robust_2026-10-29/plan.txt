Phase 3 (SOTA v7 roadmap, docs/roadmap/sota-v7-plan-2026-10.md): robust loops, on GrandTour development missions
(2026-10-29). Not for COMFORT: the deadline work is closed with submission 6. Development data only.

Why GrandTour: its development missions have a reference (the prism's position), revisits, and a frontend (HS-G15g)
of about 2 cm, so a loop must be better than that to help. The roadmap's three items are: loop registrations as
accurate as the frontend (GICP instead of NDT), loop information from the registration's Hessian instead of a
constant or the fitness, and a robust solve instead of hand-set rejection gates.

Tool: scripts/p3_grandtour_loop_errors.py. Each loop edge's translation is compared, in its first submap's frame,
with the reference's relative position of the two submaps (the reference prism positions fitted to the frontend,
the lever removed with the frontend's attitude); the frontend's own relative translation is scored the same way.
Edges whose stamps are more than 0.2 s from a reference sample are left unscored.

Baseline (baseline_ndt2_errors.csv; the runs of loops_2026-10-27/: HS-G15 frontend, walking backend preset, NDT 2 m
cells, loops after 60 m (walk60) or 20 m (walk20) of travel):
  walk20: 19 edges, 12 scored; loop error median 29.8 mm, max 1334 mm, 2 above 0.1 m; the frontend's relative error
          for the same pairs median 19.1 mm, max 50 mm; the loop is better than the frontend on 5 of 12.
  walk60: 16 edges, 13 scored; loop error median 32.8 mm, max 1334 mm, 3 above 0.1 m; frontend median 28.2 mm,
          max 40 mm; the loop is better on 5 of 13.
  The gross errors: spx-1 3-43 (1.33 m, fitness 0.77) and 3-73 (1.21 m, 0.47), con-3 18-70 (0.10 m). The drift gate
  (0.5 m + 2 % of the travel between the submaps) lets a 1.33-m error through after 64 m of travel; this frontend
  drifts 2-5 cm there. These edges are why spx-1's causal output was worse (0.0188 -> 0.0263 m).
Reading: the NDT loop edges are no more accurate than the frontend they should correct, and the gate does not stop the
gross ones. Both items of the roadmap show here directly.

Step 1 (2026-10-29): the backend inputs are recorded with HS-G15g (ws/rko_g35, label rl_g15g, KEEP_BACKEND_INPUT=1;
spx-1's is 249 MB) and the walk20 backend run on each as the baseline. spx-1's loops repeat the old ones: 3-43 again

Step 2 plan (fixed before its runs). graph_based_slam now takes gicp_max_correspondence_distance (default 30 m, the
old fixed value; lidar_slam_ros2, this commit), built into its own workspace on top of ws/slam_dev so the baseline
runner is not touched. Offline runs of graph_slam_offline_runner on the six recorded inputs, the walk20 preset with
one change each:
  ndt2    the preset (NDT 2 m cells), the baseline;
  ndt1    ndt_resolution 1.0;
  ndt05   ndt_resolution 0.5;
  gicp1   registration_method GICP, gicp_max_correspondence_distance 1.0;
  gicp05  the same with 0.5.
The initial guess of every loop registration is the frontend's relative pose, which is within a few centimetres of
the truth here, so a short correspondence distance should not cost convergence.
Reported per variant: loop edges found and scored, the median and largest loop error, how many above 0.1 m, the
frontend's error for the same pairs, the causal ATE against the frontend's.
Reading: a variant registers "as accurately as the frontend" if the median error of its scored loop edges is at
most the frontend's median for the same pairs and none of them is above 0.1 m. Such a variant goes on to the next
items (information from the registration's Hessian, a robust solve); none is adopted from this step.

Amendment (2026-10-29, before any run with it): the GICP variants made no loop edge, because the registration never
reported convergence: PCL's GICP requires the transformation to change by less than 1e-8 between iterations within
100 of them, and with the debug log every candidate on spx-1 is "rejected because registration did not converge".
graph_based_slam now takes gicp_transformation_epsilon (default 1e-8, as before). Added variants, run once the
current ones are done (on a rebuild of ws/slam_rl): gicp1e5 (GICP, 1.0 m, epsilon 1e-5) and gicp05e5 (0.5 m, 1e-5).
The reading is unchanged.

Step 3 plan (fixed before its runs): the robust solve the backend already has. loop_edge_robust_kernel_type dcs
(Dynamic Covariance Scaling, Agarwal et al. 2013) with loop_edge_robust_kernel_delta 1.0 (the default Phi) instead of
the preset's huber. With the preset's constant loop information (translation 200, rotation 1e5), a loop 1.37 m off
has chi2 about 375 and DCS scales it by 2 Phi / (Phi + chi2), about 0.005; a loop 3 cm off (chi2 about 0.2) keeps
its full weight. Variants: ndt1_dcs and ndt05_dcs (and gicp1e5_dcs if gicp1e5 finds loops). The loop measurements
do not change; what is judged is the causal output.
Reading: the backend "helps" on these missions if its mean causal ATE over the six is below the frontend's and no
mission is worse than the frontend by more than 1 mm. A variant that helps is then checked on the other development
data (MapsHD, Oxford, HILTI exp04 in chapters 25-27) before anything is adopted.

Step 2 result, PCL GICP at epsilon 1e-5 (2026-10-29; ws/slam_rl rebuilt with 75bbc6a, its ndt2 loop edges on spx-1
byte for byte the old ones): gicp1e5 1 edge on the six missions, gicp05e5 3; they are the edges of the 1e-8 runs
(the same pairs, fitness and translation), and each loop translation equals the frontend's relative translation (loop
error = frontend error: 26.4 mm; 36.7, 25.5, 29.3 mm), so the causal output is the frontend's. The epsilon was not
the cause: PCL's GICP (pclomp, BFGS) leaves the initial guess as it is; on the other candidates the solver breaks
off (an exception ends the iteration, so convergence is never set). Not pursued further.
