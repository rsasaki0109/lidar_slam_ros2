Seventeenth screen on the HS rig: the registration takes the gravity of the moving-start window (2026-10-28, written
before any of these runs).

Why: with scale and lag removed (scale_2026-10-28.txt) HS-G15g is the best or near it on most development missions,
but on the moving starts BIEVR-LIO is 2-3 mm better (arc-5, arc-4, ice-1), and our height is worse there. The walking
initialization tilts the world frame (arc-5 16 deg, arc-4 2.9, snow-3 2.8, ice-1 1.5; the test's snow-2 2.1, spx-2
2.4): the propagation and the attitude regularization keep using (0, 0, -g) in that frame. HS-G15g estimates the
gravity again after 5 s of motion but gives it to the output filter only. The fifth screen gave it to the whole LIO
(mg5) and arc-5 came out 0.0180 against 0.0152 on single runs, before noise_2026-10-28/ showed that G15c's 0.0152 was
the low end of a 0.0152-0.0185 spread (four-run mean 0.0170). This screen judges it again on four-run means.
Variants (ws/rko_g32, rko_lio eebe6e4; all with convergence_criterion 3.0e-5 (c30), 2.5e-5, 3.5e-5, 4.0e-5):
  g15g = HS-G15g (the baseline; c30 is HS-G15g itself);
  mgl5 = HS-G15g with moving_start_gravity_output_only false (the registration, the propagation and the
         regularization take the window's gravity too);
  mgl2 = the same with a 2-s window.
Static starts are untouched by these options (no window without a moving start).
Missions: the moving starts snow-3, arc-5, arc-4, ice-1, arc-6.
Reported: per mission the four runs and their mean (ATE without reference spikes), the height error, the tilt
estimated. A variant is a candidate if its mean over the five missions' four-run means is at least 0.5 mm below
HS-G15g's and no mission's four-run mean is worse than HS-G15g's by more than 10 % and 2 mm at once. All five moving
starts serve here, so a configuration from it can be checked only for doing what it says (as decision_rule_G15f.txt
states); the test's snow-2 and spx-2 are the first independent evidence.
